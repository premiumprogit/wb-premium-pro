Costs

What a Bathroom Remodel Costs in Lynnfield, MA

Bathroom pricing depends far more on scope than on square footage. A small bathroom where the toilet and shower move can cost more than a large one where every pipe stays in place. As a guide based on the bathrooms we build on the North Shore:

  • Cosmetic refresh: about $12,000 to $18,000. New vanity and top, toilet, fixtures, lighting, mirror and paint, with the existing layout and most of the existing tile in good condition.
  • Full gut remodel: about $18,000 to $28,000. Down to the studs, new waterproofing, new tile on floor and walls, a walk-in shower or new tub, vanity and fixtures.
  • Primary suite and larger layouts: up to about $32,000. Double vanities, larger custom showers, heated floors, moved plumbing and more detailed tile work.

Plan a contingency on top of the number you are comfortable with. Bathrooms surface more surprises than any other room once the walls are open, especially in older homes, and a buffer keeps the project moving instead of stopping for a hard conversation in the middle of the job. Your real number comes from a walkthrough. We look at the framing, subfloor, plumbing and venting first, then send a written, itemized estimate so you can compare it line by line with any other quote.

A common question is whether $10,000 is enough. For a light refresh where the tile and plumbing are in good shape, sometimes it comes close. If the shower area needs new tile, the subfloor around the toilet is soft, or the layout changes, it usually is not, and a quote that low for that scope is often leaving out waterproofing, permits or repairs.

Timeline

How Long a Lynnfield Bathroom Remodel Takes

A typical bathroom remodel takes about two to four weeks on site. A straightforward tub-to-shower conversion or a refresh can finish in about two weeks, while a full gut with plumbing changes, custom tile and a primary-suite layout runs closer to three or four. Planning and ordering happen before that, so the crew is never waiting on a vanity or a glass door in the middle of the job.

Here is how the weeks usually break down. The first days are demolition and protection, the loudest part. Then framing repairs, plumbing and electrical rough-in and the inspections that go with them. Next comes the waterproofing and the backer board, then tile, which is the longest visible stage because each layer needs time to set. The last days are the vanity, toilet, glass, fixtures, paint and a final walkthrough with a punch list. If this is the only full bathroom in the house, tell us at the estimate and we will plan the sequence to keep a working toilet available as long as possible.

Permits

Permits and Inspections for a Bathroom in Lynnfield

Any bathroom remodel that touches plumbing, electrical or framing needs a permit, and in Massachusetts the plumbing and electrical permits are pulled by the licensed trades doing that work. In Lynnfield, permits come through the town's building department, and the rough-in work is inspected before the walls are closed.

As a licensed Massachusetts general contractor, we coordinate the permits and inspections as part of the project, so you are not chasing paperwork or scheduling inspectors yourself. Be cautious if a contractor suggests skipping the permit to save time or money. Unpermitted plumbing and electrical work can create problems when you sell the house, and an inspection is a built-in quality check that protects you.

Waterproofing

Waterproofing Decides How Long Your Bathroom Lasts

Tile and grout are not waterproof. What keeps water out of your framing and away from the ceiling below is the layer underneath. Most bathroom failures we are called to fix come from tile installed directly over drywall, or from a shower pan that was never sealed or sloped correctly. The result is loose tile, mold behind the wall and stains on the ceiling of the room below.

We build showers on a continuous waterproof membrane over a proper backer, with a base sloped to the drain and every seam, niche and corner sealed. The bathroom gets a correctly sized exhaust fan that vents outside, not into the attic, which matters in New England winters when cold attics turn trapped moisture into frost and mold. It is the part of the job you will never see again once the tile is up, and the reason we do it ourselves instead of hurrying to the finish work.

Showers

Walk-In Showers and Tub-to-Shower Conversions

Replacing a tub you rarely use with a walk-in shower is one of the most requested bathroom projects we build. A well-designed shower with a bench and a niche is easier to step into, easier to clean and more useful day to day. We remove the old tub, reframe and waterproof the new shower base, run any plumbing changes and tile the walls and floor.

  • A linear drain lets you run one continuous floor tile into a curbless or low-curb shower.
  • Plan the niche height and bench location before framing, not after the walls are closed.
  • A fixed glass panel is simpler to keep clean than a hinged door and fits many layouts.

If it is the only tub in the house, we talk through resale before you decide. Some buyers with young children still look for one tub somewhere in the home, so many Lynnfield families keep a tub in the hall bath and convert the primary.

Tile

Tile Work That Looks Custom, Not Rushed

Tile is where a bathroom looks finished or looks rushed. Straight grout lines, tile that lines up across the floor and walls, clean cuts around fixtures and edges that are finished instead of left raw are all details people notice the moment they walk in.

Porcelain is the most durable choice for floors and showers, and large-format tile means fewer grout lines and less cleaning. Natural stone looks beautiful but needs sealing and more care. Mosaic floors in a shower give better grip. We lay out the tile before setting it, so cuts land in the corners and not in the middle of the wall, and we use the right setting materials for each surface. If you want a feature wall, a niche in an accent tile or a patterned floor, we plan it on paper first so you can see it before it is permanent.

Vanities and Fixtures

Vanities, Countertops and Fixtures That Fit the Room

The vanity sets the style and the storage of the whole bathroom. In smaller Lynnfield bathrooms, a floating vanity shows more floor and makes the room feel larger, while drawers usually store more than cabinet doors. Plywood boxes hold up to bathroom humidity better than particleboard. Quartz tops resist stains and need no sealing.

For fixtures, choose one finish and keep it consistent across the faucet, shower trim, towel bars and hardware. Pressure-balanced or thermostatic shower valves keep the water temperature steady when someone flushes or runs a sink. Comfort-height toilets are easier for most adults, and a handheld shower on a slide bar helps with cleaning and with bathing kids or pets. We help you pick what is worth spending on and where you can save without regret.

Small Bathrooms

Small Bathroom Remodels in Lynnfield

Many bathrooms in Lynnfield capes, ranches and older colonials are small, and that is fine. Good planning matters more than square footage. The moves that give the most space back are usually simple:

  • A floating vanity and a recessed medicine cabinet instead of a mirror that sticks out.
  • One continuous floor tile running into the shower, so the eye does not stop at a curb.
  • A pocket door where a swinging door blocks the toilet or the vanity.
  • Light walls with one warm material, such as a wood vanity, so the room does not feel cold.
  • Layered lighting: a ceiling light, a light at the mirror and a light in the shower.

We often mark the new layout on the floor with tape during the walkthrough, so you can stand in the room and feel the clearances before anything is ordered.

Primary Bathrooms

Primary Suite and Master Bathroom Remodels

In larger Lynnfield homes, the primary bathroom is often the project homeowners have postponed the longest. These remodels usually involve a larger custom shower, a double vanity, better storage, heated floors and sometimes a freestanding tub. Because they touch more plumbing and electrical and often change the layout, they take more planning and sit at the upper end of the price range.

We start with how two people use the room every morning: who needs which sink, where towels and toiletries live, how the light works at the mirror and whether the toilet should have its own space. A heated floor makes a real difference in a Massachusetts winter, and good ventilation protects the investment for years. You see the plan and the written price before demolition, so there are no surprises about what the finished room will be.

Older Homes

What We Often Find Behind the Walls in Older Homes

Opening a bathroom wall in an older Lynnfield home often reveals conditions that a quick quote never mentions. The most common ones we see on the North Shore:

  • Old galvanized supply lines that restrict water pressure and corrode from the inside.
  • Hidden water damage and soft subfloor around tubs and toilets.
  • Outdated wiring and missing ground-fault protection near water.
  • Exhaust fans that vent into the attic instead of outside, or no fan at all.
  • Framing that was cut or notched by past plumbing work.

When the walkthrough shows the signs, we price the likely repairs up front. If something new turns up once the walls are open, we explain it with photos and a price before any extra work is approved. That is the purpose of the contingency, and it is also why one general contractor handling framing, plumbing coordination and finish work keeps the project from stalling between trades.

Accessibility

Safer Bathrooms for Every Stage of Life

A remodel is the right moment to plan for the future, even if no one needs it today. Adding solid blocking inside the walls costs very little during framing and lets you install grab bars later without opening the wall again. A curbless or low-curb shower entry, a built-in bench, slip-resistant floor tile, a handheld shower and comfort-height fixtures make a bathroom easier to use for everyone, from young kids to visiting parents.

Many Lynnfield homeowners planning to stay in their homes for the long term ask for these details now, so the bathroom works for the next twenty years without looking like a medical space. Good design makes them almost invisible.

Hiring Guide

Questions to Ask Any Bathroom Contractor in Lynnfield

Whether you hire us or someone else, these questions protect you and make quotes easier to compare:

  • Are you licensed and insured in Massachusetts, and will you show proof before the job starts?
  • What exactly is included, and what is an allowance that can change?
  • Which waterproofing system will you use in the shower, and how do you test it?

  • How do you handle hidden damage found after demolition, and how is it priced?
  • Is the payment schedule tied to completed stages of the work?
