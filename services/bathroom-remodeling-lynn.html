Bathroom Remodeling in Lynn's Older and Multi-Family Homes

Lynn is a city of older housing, and a large share of the bathrooms we remodel here live inside triple-deckers, two-families and homes that were built long before modern plumbing codes. That history is charming from the street and unforgiving behind the walls. Cast iron waste stacks, galvanized supply lines that have narrowed to a trickle, undersized venting, and framing that has settled out of square are all things we routinely find the moment demolition starts. A contractor who only knows new construction gets surprised by these homes. We plan for them.

In a multi-family, the added challenge is that you are never working on a bathroom in isolation. There are tenants above and below, a single water shutoff that serves the whole building, and stacked plumbing where the bathroom you are gutting sits directly over someone else's kitchen. Before we touch a pipe we map the shutoffs, coordinate access with the units we are not working in, and protect their ceilings and floors. When a leak in a Lynn triple-decker means water in three apartments, that coordination is not optional, and it is exactly the kind of ownership a single accountable general contractor exists to provide.

There is also a resale and rental angle unique to a rental-heavy city. An unpermitted bathroom, or one where the plumbing was hacked in without inspection, can stall a sale, complicate an insurance claim or hold up a certificate of occupancy. Because we pull the City of Lynn permits and run the licensed trades through inspection, the finished bathroom is documented and defensible, which protects the value of a two-family or three-family you may want to sell or refinance down the line.

Waterproofing: The Part of Your Lynn Bathroom That Actually Matters

Almost every failed bathroom traces back to one thing, and it is not the tile you can see, it is the waterproofing you cannot. Tile is not waterproof. Grout is not waterproof. The system behind them has to be, and in far too many bathrooms it simply is not there. A tiler sets beautiful tile directly over green drywall, the shower looks perfect for a year, and then moisture wicks into the wall cavity and quietly feeds rot and mold until a soft floor or a stained ceiling below gives it away.

We build the shower as a waterproof system from the base up. The pan is sloped correctly to the drain so water actually leaves the shower instead of pooling, a continuous waterproof membrane wraps the base and climbs the walls, and every seam, corner and pipe penetration is sealed. Only then does the tile go on, as the finish surface it was always meant to be, not as the last line of defense against water. In an older Lynn home where the wall cavity may already carry decades of moisture, we also check and dry the framing before we close it up, so we are not sealing a new bathroom over an old problem.

This is the work a homeowner never sees and never gets to inspect after the fact, which is precisely why it is the first thing cut-rate crews skip. It is also why we treat it as the foundation of the entire project. A bathroom that is waterproofed correctly is boring in the best way: it just stays dry, year after year, while the room around it earns its keep.

Tub-to-Shower Conversions in Lynn

One of our most requested Lynn projects is converting a cramped, dated tub into a larger, easier-to-use walk-in shower. It is popular for a reason. Many Lynn bathrooms are narrow, and an old alcove tub eats space that a curbed or curbless shower can open up dramatically. For an aging homeowner who wants to stay in the home, a low-threshold shower with a bench and a grab bar is far safer than stepping over a tub wall every day.

The conversion is more than pulling out the tub. We remove the old unit, reframe the opening, rebuild and waterproof a proper shower base, adjust the plumbing for the new valve and drain location, and tile the walls and floor. Done right, you gain usable space, a modern look and a bathroom that is genuinely easier to live with. The one caution we always raise is resale: if the tub coming out is the only bathtub in the house, we talk it through first, because many buyers still want at least one tub. In a multi-bath home or a rental where a shower reads as an upgrade, it is usually an easy call.

Ventilation, Mold and Keeping a Lynn Bathroom Healthy

A beautiful bathroom that cannot get rid of moisture will not stay beautiful, and in coastal, humid Lynn that pressure is real year-round. Steam that has nowhere to go condenses on cold surfaces and feeds mildew on ceilings, in corners and behind trim. A lot of older bathrooms either have no exhaust fan at all or have one that vents into an attic or a wall cavity, which just moves the moisture problem somewhere you cannot see it.

We size the exhaust fan to the room and vent it fully to the exterior, so the humid air actually leaves the house. Combined with correct waterproofing and the right paint, a properly ventilated bathroom sheds moisture instead of trapping it, which keeps mold from ever getting a foothold. It is a small line item that quietly protects the entire investment, and it is one of the first things we check when a homeowner calls us to fix a previous remodel that started peeling or staining within a year.

How long does a bathroom remodel take in Lynn?

A typical Lynn bathroom remodel takes about two to four weeks on site. A straightforward tub-to-shower conversion or a refresh can finish in two weeks, while a full gut with plumbing relocation, custom tile and a primary suite layout runs three to four weeks. Many Lynn homes are close to a century old, so opening a wall can reveal galvanized supply lines or a rotted subfloor, and that adds a few days we account for up front rather than as a surprise change order.

Do I need a permit to remodel a bathroom in Lynn, MA?

Yes. Any bathroom remodel in Lynn that touches plumbing, electrical or framing needs a permit from the City of Lynn Inspectional Services, and the plumbing and electrical work must be pulled by the licensed trades doing the work. As a licensed Massachusetts general contractor we handle the permits and coordinate the inspections, which matters even more in a multi-family or rental where an unpermitted bathroom can hold up a sale or a certificate of occupancy.

Can you remodel a bathroom in an old or multi-family Lynn home?

Yes, that is most of the bathroom work we do in Lynn. Older triple-deckers and two-families have their own quirks: tight stacked plumbing, tenants above and below, narrow bathrooms and framing that is not square. We plan the sequence around a shared water shutoff, protect the units we are not working in, and reframe and waterproof so the finished bathroom does not leak into the apartment below. That coordination is exactly why hiring one accountable general contractor beats stringing together separate trades.

Will my Lynn bathroom leak or grow mold after the remodel?

Not when the waterproofing is done right. Most bathroom failures come from tile installed over drywall or a shower pan that was never sealed correctly, and in an older Lynn home the wall cavity may already have taken on moisture. We build showers on a waterproof membrane and a properly sloped base, seal every seam and penetration, and add correctly sized ventilation, so the bathroom stays dry behind the tile, not just on the surface.
