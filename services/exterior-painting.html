Our Exterior Painting Process, Step by Step

Every exterior painting project we run on the North Shore follows the same disciplined sequence, because the order of the work is what determines whether the finish lasts a decade or peels in two seasons. We do not shortcut the front-end labor to hit a lower price, because the prep is the job. Here is exactly how your house gets painted.

First we walk the property with you and give a free written estimate that spells out the surfaces, the prep, the number of coats and the price, so nothing is a surprise. On day one we mask and protect landscaping, walkways, windows, light fixtures and anything that should not catch overspray or drips, then we power wash the entire exterior to strip away dirt, chalk, pollen and mildew, because fresh paint will not bond to a dirty wall. We let the surfaces dry fully before a hand touches them.

Next comes the part cheaper crews skip: scraping every inch of loose and failing paint, sanding rough edges smooth, and feathering the transitions so the repaint reads flat rather than patchy. We prime all bare wood and any stained or bleeding spots with the right primer for the substrate, then caulk and seal every gap around trim, corner boards, windows and joints, because water getting behind the paint is the number one cause of early failure on the North Shore. Any rotted trim, siding or fascia gets repaired or replaced before we paint, which is where being a general contractor pays off. Only then do we apply two full coats of exterior-grade finish, cutting in by hand and back-rolling or spraying for even coverage, and we finish with a walkthrough so you sign off on a house that is protected, not just recolored.

Paint and Materials Built for Massachusetts Weather

The North Shore puts an exterior finish through everything: brutal freeze-thaw cycles in winter, strong UV in summer, coastal humidity and salt air near the water, and long stretches of damp in spring and fall. Ordinary interior-grade or bargain paint cracks, chalks and fades fast under that kind of exposure. We use premium exterior-grade coatings formulated for New England, with UV resistance to hold color, flexibility to move through freeze-thaw without cracking, and mildew inhibitors that matter in our humid, shaded yards.

The right product also depends on the substrate. Wood clapboard, cedar shingles, fiber cement, aluminum and vinyl siding all take different primers and finishes, and using the wrong system is a common reason a repaint fails early. We match primer and topcoat to your siding, spot-prime bare and stained areas correctly, and choose a sheen that balances durability with how the house should look. For homeowners who want it, we can also talk through solid-body and semi-transparent staining on decks, shingles and fences as a longer-wearing alternative to paint on certain surfaces.

Common Exterior Painting Mistakes We Never Make

Most of the peeling, blistering and premature failure we get called to fix comes down to a handful of avoidable mistakes. Painting over dirt or mildew instead of power washing first is the classic one, because nothing bonds to a chalky, grimy surface. Skipping primer on bare wood is another, since the topcoat has nothing to grip and lifts within a season or two. Painting over damp wood or ahead of rain traps moisture that pushes the film right off the house.

We also see crews leave gaps uncaulked so water runs behind the finish, apply a single thin coat and call it two, or paint over rotted trim to hide the damage rather than fix it. On the North Shore, painting too late in the season when nights turn cold means the paint never cures properly. We avoid every one of these by prepping fully, priming and caulking, applying two true coats, repairing wood before we paint, and only working in the right conditions. That discipline is the difference between a job that looks good on day one and a job that still looks good ten years later.

Siding Types We Paint and How We Handle Each

North Shore homes are built from a wide range of exterior materials, and each one needs its own approach if the paint is going to last. Wood clapboard and cedar shingle siding, common on the older homes in Salem, Beverly and Marblehead, need careful scraping, sanding and spot priming, and cedar in particular needs a stain-blocking primer to stop tannin bleed. Fiber cement siding holds paint extremely well but still needs clean, dry surfaces and the right primer at cut edges. Aluminum siding that has oxidized to a chalky finish must be washed thoroughly and often primed with a bonding primer, and vinyl siding, while frequently left unpainted, can be repainted successfully with vinyl-safe formulations that will not warp the material in the sun.

We identify your siding type during the estimate and spec the entire system around it, from wash and prep through primer and topcoat, so you are never getting a one-size approach forced onto a surface it was never meant for. That is a large part of why our finishes hold when others fail, and it is a benefit of hiring a general contractor who understands the whole building envelope rather than a crew that paints everything the same way.

How Weather and Timing Affect Your Exterior Paint Job

Massachusetts weather does not only affect how long paint lasts, it affects when and how we can apply it in the first place. Paint needs the right temperature and humidity to cure, generally above 50 degrees and dropping no lower overnight, with dry surfaces and no rain in the immediate forecast. Paint over damp wood, or ahead of a storm, and the moisture trapped underneath will lift the film off the house within a season. Paint too late in the fall when nights turn cold and the finish never fully cures.

We plan every North Shore project around real conditions rather than a rigid calendar, watching the forecast and sequencing the walls so we are always painting surfaces that are dry and stable. If the weather turns, we protect the work and pause rather than push through and compromise the finish. That patience is unglamorous, but it is one of the quiet reasons our exterior paint lasts the full 8 to 10 years.

Exterior Painting Versus Staining for North Shore Homes

Not every exterior surface is best served by paint. On decks, cedar shingles, fences and some natural-wood elements, a quality stain can be the smarter long-term choice because it penetrates the wood rather than sitting on top of it, which means it wears by fading gradually instead of peeling and cracking. Solid-body stains give you color and coverage close to paint with easier future recoats, while semi-transparent stains let the grain show through for a more natural, coastal look that suits a lot of North Shore properties.

During your estimate we will tell you honestly where paint is the right call and where a stain will serve you better and cost less to maintain over time. Siding bodies and trim almost always want a proper primer-and-paint system, but a weathered deck or a run of cedar shingles may be a better candidate for stain. Matching the coating to the surface, rather than defaulting to paint everywhere, is part of getting a result that both looks right and lasts.

Maintenance That Extends the Life of Your Paint

A good exterior paint job is not maintenance-free, but it asks very little. A gentle rinse of the walls once a year keeps dirt, pollen and mildew from building up and dulling the finish, which matters most on shaded, north-facing and coastal walls where growth takes hold fastest. Keeping gutters clear and downspouts directed away from the siding prevents the constant wetting that breaks paint down at the base of walls. Trimming shrubs back off the house improves airflow and keeps moisture from sitting against the finish.

We walk you through the simple upkeep that fits your specific house at the final walkthrough, and we are always available to touch up a weather-worn spot or refresh a high-exposure wall down the road. Because we keep records of the exact colors and products we used, matching a future touch-up is straightforward rather than a guessing game.

North Shore Exterior Painting FAQ

How much does exterior painting cost on the North Shore, MA?

Most exterior painting projects on the North Shore run $4,500 to $12,000 for an average single-family home, and larger or badly weathered houses go higher. Price depends on square footage, how many stories, the amount of scraping, sanding and carpentry repair the siding and trim need, and access around the property. A small ranch with sound siding sits near the low end, while a big Victorian with lots of trim, peeling paint and second-story work lands higher. Your written, itemized estimate is free, so you see the real number after we walk the property, not a guess over the phone.

How long does exterior paint last in Massachusetts?

A properly prepped exterior paint job lasts 8 to 10 years on the North Shore, and sometimes longer on protected walls. The number depends far more on prep than on the paint itself. Homes that get power washed, scraped, sanded, primed on bare wood and caulked before painting hold their finish for a decade, while jobs painted over dirt, loose paint and open gaps can fail in two or three seasons. Sun-facing and weather-facing walls always wear faster than sheltered ones, which is why we spec the prep to the wall.

What is the best time of year to paint a house on the North Shore?

The best exterior painting window on the North Shore runs from late spring through early fall, roughly May to October, when daytime temperatures stay reliably above 50 degrees and overnight temperatures do not dip too low. Paint needs stable, dry, mild conditions to cure and bond correctly. We watch the forecast closely and never paint over damp surfaces or ahead of rain, because trapped moisture is the fastest way to cause peeling. Early booking in spring is smart, since the good-weather calendar fills up quickly.

Do you scrape and prep before painting, or just paint over the old finish?

We always prep first. Every exterior job starts with power washing to remove dirt, chalk and mildew, then hand scraping and sanding all loose and failing paint, priming any bare wood, and caulking gaps around trim, windows and joints. Paint bonds to a clean, sound surface and fails over old failures, so skipping prep is the single biggest reason cheap paint jobs peel. The prep is most of the labor and most of the reason our finishes last.

Can you repair rotted wood, trim and siding before painting?

Yes. Because we are a full general contractor and not just a painting crew, we handle the carpentry that other painters skip. Before we paint we replace rotted trim, repair or swap damaged clapboards and shingles, re-seat loose boards and rebuild soffits and fascia where needed. Painting over rot only hides it for a season, so fixing the wood first is what makes the finish last and protects the structure underneath.

We carry the coverage that protects you and your property during ladder and staging work, and we can provide proof of license and insurance before we start. Working with licensed, insured painters matters most on exterior jobs because of the height, staging and liability involved.

We paint homes across the North Shore from our Peabody base, including Peabody, Salem, Beverly, Marblehead, Swampscott, Danvers, Lynnfield, Saugus, Lynn, Revere, Wakefield, Stoneham and the surrounding towns. Being local means we know the housing stock, the weather exposure and the local permit process, and we show up on time because the job is a short drive away.
