Types of Additions

The Home Additions We Build Across the North Shore

Home additions North Shore MA homeowners ask us about come in every shape, from a few extra feet to a whole new floor, and the right one depends on your lot, your house and how you actually live. As a full general contractor we build all of them, and part of our job at the estimate stage is helping you pick the addition that gets you the space you need for the money you want to spend. Here are the additions we build most often on the North Shore.

Bump-Out Additions

A small, cantilevered or foundation-supported extension of a few feet that widens a cramped kitchen, enlarges a bathroom, or adds a breakfast nook. The most affordable way to gain function without a full addition.

Full Room Additions

A complete new ground-floor room on its own foundation: a family room, a first-floor bedroom, a home office or a sunroom. Adds real square footage and changes how the whole floor lives.

Second-Story Additions

Building up rather than out when your lot is tight. A new second floor or a partial upper level adds bedrooms and baths while keeping your yard, and it is one of the strongest ways to raise home value.

In-Law & Accessory Dwelling Units

A self-contained suite with its own kitchen, bath and entrance for aging parents, an adult child or a rental. We design ADUs to comply with the evolving Massachusetts rules and blend into your home.

Garage Conversions

Turning an underused attached garage into finished living space, a suite or a studio, or building above the garage. Often the cheapest square footage on the lot because the shell already exists.

Primary Suite Additions

A new primary bedroom with a walk-in closet and a full bathroom remodeling level en suite, giving older North Shore homes the owner retreat they were never built with.

Kitchen Extensions

Pushing the kitchen out to create an open, eat-in space, tied straight into a full kitchen remodeling so the new footprint and the new kitchen are planned as one project.

Sunrooms & Mudrooms

A light-filled sunroom off the back or a hardworking mudroom off the side entry. Smaller additions that solve everyday problems and add comfort year round.

Our Design-Build Process for a North Shore Home Addition

A home addition is the most involved project most homeowners ever take on, and the difference between a smooth one and a stressful one is almost always the planning. Because we handle design coordination, permitting and construction under one roof, you are never the person stuck translating between an architect, a town office and a builder who each blame the other. Here is how a North Shore addition actually runs with us, step by step, so there are no surprises about who does what and when.

It starts with an on-site walkthrough where we listen to what you are trying to solve, more bedrooms, a bigger kitchen, a place for a parent, and look hard at the house and the lot: where the addition can go, how it can tie into the existing structure, and what your zoning allows. From there we move into design and engineering, developing the layout, elevations and, when a structural change requires it, stamped plans from a structural engineer. Only once the design is set do we produce a written, itemized proposal, so the number you see reflects a real plan rather than a rough guess.

With the plan approved, we handle the local building permit and any zoning relief, then break ground: foundation or footings, framing tied into your existing home, roofing and dry-in so the structure is weather-tight, then the trades, insulation, drywall and finish work. We keep you updated at each stage, pass every inspection, and finish with a walkthrough together before we call the job done. One point of contact carries the whole thing, from the first sketch to the final coat of paint.

Foundations and Tying New Structure Into an Existing Home

The part of an addition nobody sees is the part that determines whether it lasts: the foundation and the connection to your existing home. A new addition needs a foundation that matches how your house is built and how the local frost line behaves, which on the North Shore means footings set below the frost depth so nothing heaves in a hard winter. Depending on the addition that might be a full basement or crawl space, a frost-protected slab, or piers and a beam for a bump-out or sunroom. We size the foundation to the load and the soil rather than to whatever is quickest.

Tying new framing into an existing house is where craftsmanship matters most, especially in older North Shore homes where nothing is perfectly plumb or level after a hundred winters. We connect new floor systems to old at the right height so your floors flow without a step or a hump, marry new walls to existing framing so there are no cracks down the seam later, and integrate the new roof into the old with proper flashing and valleys so it never leaks. A cheap addition looks bolted on because someone rushed these connections. A good one disappears into the house because we did not.

Matching Rooflines, Siding and Windows So the Addition Looks Original

An addition that clearly reads as new hurts both your enjoyment of the home and its resale value, so getting the exterior to match is not a finishing touch, it is a design decision we make from day one. That means matching the roof pitch and the depth of the overhangs, carrying the siding profile and exposure across so the lines run continuous, and choosing windows that match the style, proportions and grille pattern of the ones you already have. On the North Shore, where clapboard, shingle and classic New England detailing are everywhere, a mismatched addition stands out immediately.

Older homes make this harder and more rewarding. Original trim profiles, window casings and moldings are often no longer sold off the shelf, so we replicate them with custom carpentry and trim so the new work blends seamlessly into the old. Where siding has faded over decades, we plan how to feather new material in or refinish adjacent areas so the transition is not jarring. The finished addition should leave a neighbor unsure whether it was there all along, and that seamlessness is exactly what protects your investment when it comes time to sell.

Permits, Zoning, Setbacks and Inspections in North Shore Towns

Every home addition in Massachusetts needs a building permit, and the plumbing, electrical and gas work must be done by licensed trades and inspected. On top of that sits zoning, and this is where additions get complicated. Each North Shore town, Peabody, Salem, Beverly, Marblehead, Swampscott, Danvers, Lynnfield and the rest, sets its own rules for how close you can build to a property line (setbacks), how much of the lot the structures can cover (lot coverage), and how tall the building can be. If your addition stays inside those limits, it is generally allowed by right and moves faster. If it crosses a setback or exceeds coverage, you may need a variance or special permit from the local zoning board.

Knowing which category your project falls into before you spend money on design is exactly the kind of thing an experienced local contractor saves you from learning the hard way. We check the zoning early, tell you honestly if a variance is likely and what that adds to the timeline, and design within the rules whenever we can so your addition is by-right. When engineering or stamped plans are required for a structural change, we coordinate that too. Then we pull the permits, post them, and schedule the framing, plumbing, electrical, insulation and final inspections so each stage is signed off properly. You never have to chase the town yourself, and you get an addition that is fully legal and clean at resale.

How an Addition Adds Value and Square Footage Cheaper Than Moving

For a lot of North Shore families, the real question is not whether they need more space, it is whether to add it or to move. When you run the numbers, adding often wins. A move carries a realtor commission, closing costs on both ends, moving expenses, and usually a higher mortgage rate on the new purchase, and on the North Shore you are also paying the market premium on a move-in-ready home with the space you want. Put all of that against the cost of a well-planned addition and staying put frequently comes out ahead, and you keep the neighborhood, the schools, the yard and the home your family already loves.

A thoughtful addition also builds equity. Adding a bedroom and bath, a proper primary suite, or turning a cramped floor plan into an open, functional one raises what your home is worth, and additions that add usable, well-integrated square footage typically return a strong share of their cost at resale. The additions that pay off best are the ones that fix a real deficiency in the house, a missing bedroom, only one full bath, no first-floor living, rather than adding space no buyer values. Part of what we do at the estimate is help you aim the money where it both improves your daily life and holds its value.

Timelines and Living in the Home During Construction

Once the design is final and the permits are in hand, a North Shore addition runs roughly 3 to 6 months on site, with a simple bump-out finishing in 6 to 10 weeks and a large second-story addition taking longer because of the temporary roof, the structural work and a full interior fit-out. Ahead of that, budget 1 to 3 months for design, engineering and the local permit process, and remember that any needed zoning relief adds to the front end. We give you a realistic schedule in your written proposal and hold to it, because one crew runs the whole job rather than juggling your project against others.

Most families stay in their home through an addition, and we plan the project so you can. We seal off the work area with dust barriers, protect floors and stairs along the entire path, and phase the work so we do not open your existing house to the weather until the new structure is framed and dried in. There are short, noisier stretches, breaking through an exterior wall to connect the addition, rerouting utilities, tying into your kitchen, and we schedule those tightly and warn you ahead of time. Each day ends with a clean, swept site rather than a construction zone you have to live around. For a large second-story project, some homeowners choose to stay elsewhere during framing, and we give you a straight answer about what to expect for your specific job.

Accessory Dwelling Units and the Changing Rules in Massachusetts

Accessory dwelling units, small independent living spaces with their own kitchen, bath and entrance, have become one of the fastest-growing reasons North Shore homeowners add on. They serve aging parents who want to stay close but independent, adult children who need an affordable landing spot, or owners looking for rental income to offset a mortgage. Massachusetts has moved to make ADUs far easier to build, allowing them by right in single-family zones across the state, which has opened the door for many homeowners who could not previously add a second unit.

That said, each North Shore town still applies its own rules on ADU size, parking, entrances and setbacks, and the details keep evolving as towns update their bylaws to match the state law. An ADU can be built as an attached addition, created above a garage, or carved out of existing space and finished properly. Because the requirements shift and vary from Peabody to Beverly to Marblehead, this is exactly the kind of project where a local general contractor who follows the rules is worth having. We design the unit to comply, keep it in character with your home and neighborhood, and handle the permitting so your ADU is legal and built to last.

Bump-Out Additions: The Smart, Affordable Extension

Not every space problem needs a full addition. A bump-out extends one wall by a few feet, just enough to widen a galley kitchen into an eat-in, turn a cramped bathroom into a real one, or carve out a breakfast nook or a home-office alcove. Because a small bump-out can sometimes be cantilevered off the existing floor framing rather than needing a full new foundation, it is often the most cost-effective way to gain the exact function you are missing without the price of a full room addition. For many North Shore homes, a well-placed bump-out solves the daily frustration for a fraction of the cost.

The craft in a bump-out is making a small addition feel intentional rather than tacked on. We match the roofline, siding and windows exactly as we would on a large addition, so the extra few feet read as original. When a bump-out ties into a kitchen or bath, we plan it alongside the kitchen remodeling or bathroom work so the new space and the finished room land as one seamless project.

Second-Story Additions When Your Lot Is Tight

On the North Shore, where lots are often small and setbacks limit how far out you can build, the answer is frequently to build up rather than out. A second-story addition, or a partial upper level, can add the bedrooms and full baths a growing family needs while keeping every foot of your yard. It is one of the strongest ways to add real square footage and value on a constrained lot, and for many older single-story or one-and-a-half-story North Shore homes it transforms the house entirely.

Building up is more involved than building out because it usually requires verifying that the existing foundation and walls can carry the new load, adding a staircase, and protecting the home under a temporary roof while the new level goes up. That is exactly why it belongs with a general contractor who can coordinate the structural engineering and the framing as one job. We plan the sequence to keep your home weather-tight and to shorten the disruptive framing phase as much as possible.

Garage Conversions and Space You Already Own

The cheapest square footage on many North Shore lots is space you already have. Converting an underused attached garage into a finished family room, a home office, a guest suite or an ADU means the shell, the roof and often the foundation already exist, so the project skips some of the most expensive parts of a ground-up addition. The work is in insulating and finishing to living-space standards, adding heat and proper egress, upgrading the electrical, and replacing the garage door with a wall and windows that match the house.

Building above the garage is another strong option, adding a suite or a bonus room over the existing footprint without giving up parking. As with any conversion, the details matter: it has to be permitted, insulated and finished so it is comfortable year round and legal at resale, not a cold, half-finished bonus space. We handle the conversion end to end so the finished room feels like it was always part of the home.

In-Law Suites and Multigenerational Living

More North Shore families are choosing to keep parents or adult children close under one roof, and an in-law suite is how they do it comfortably. Whether it is a full accessory dwelling unit with its own kitchen and entrance or a suite with a bedroom, a full bath and a sitting area that connects to the main house, the goal is the same: independence and privacy for everyone, without the cost and heartache of a separate home or a care facility. A first-floor in-law suite also solves the stairs problem for aging parents.

Designing multigenerational space well takes thought about entrances, sound separation, accessibility and how much the two households share. We plan the suite around how your family actually lives, comply with the local rules on accessory units, and build it so it can flex over time, serving a parent now and becoming a rental or a returning-child space later. It is one of the additions that changes a family's life the most.

Sunrooms, Mudrooms and Everyday-Comfort Additions

Some of the most-loved additions are the smaller ones that fix an everyday annoyance. A sunroom off the back gives you a bright, three-season place to sit that connects the house to the yard, and with the right insulation and heat it can be a true year-round room in the New England climate. A mudroom off the side or back entry gives a North Shore family the drop zone it desperately needs for boots, coats, sports gear and the sand and slush that come with living near the coast and through real winters.

These additions are smaller in scope but no less deserving of real craftsmanship. We match the exterior so they never look like an afterthought, and we design the interior, built-in benches and cubbies in a mudroom, plenty of glass and durable flooring in a sunroom, around exactly how you will use the space. Often they pair with a fresh coat of interior painting so the new room ties cleanly into the rooms around it.

We also sequence the work to limit how long your home is exposed. We do not break through an exterior wall until the new structure is framed and dried in, so your house is never open to the weather longer than it must be. North Shore Home Additions FAQ

How much do home additions cost on the North Shore of MA?

Most home additions on the North Shore run roughly $30,000 to $350,000, because the term covers everything from a small bump-out to a full second story. A bump-out to widen a kitchen or bath usually lands around $30,000 to $75,000, a full single-room ground-floor addition runs about $90,000 to $200,000, and a second-story addition or an in-law suite with its own kitchen and bath commonly runs $180,000 to $350,000 or more. Older North Shore homes can carry a little extra to tie new framing into settled structure and to match existing rooflines, siding and windows. Your written, itemized estimate is free so you see the real number for your house before any work begins.

How long does a home addition take on the North Shore?

A typical North Shore home addition takes about 3 to 6 months on site once the design is final and permits are in hand, plus roughly 1 to 3 months up front for design, engineering and the local permit process. A simple bump-out can finish in 6 to 10 weeks, while a large second-story addition runs longer because of the temporary roof, structural work and full interior fit-out. Because one accountable crew runs the whole job, there are no gaps waiting on a subcontractor who is busy elsewhere, which is a big part of why our additions finish close to the promised date.

Do I need a permit and a variance for a home addition in Massachusetts?

Yes, every home addition in Massachusetts needs a building permit, and the plumbing, electrical and gas work must be done by licensed trades and inspected. Whether you also need zoning relief depends on your lot: if the addition stays within your town's setbacks, lot coverage and height limits, it is usually by-right, but if it encroaches on a setback or exceeds coverage you may need a variance or special permit from the local zoning board. As your licensed general contractor we check the zoning early, pull the permits, coordinate engineering when a structural change requires stamped plans, and schedule every inspection so the addition is signed off to code.

Will the addition match the rest of my house?

That is the whole point of a well-built addition, and it is where many jobs fall short. We match the roofline pitch and overhangs, the siding profile and exposure, the window style and trim, and the interior floor height and moldings so the new space reads as original rather than bolted on. In older North Shore homes that often means custom carpentry to blend new trim into old, and careful planning so the new roof ties cleanly into the existing one without leaks. Done right, an addition should make people wonder whether the house was always that size.

Can an addition add square footage cheaper than moving?

For many North Shore families, yes. When you factor in a realtor commission, closing costs, moving expenses, a higher mortgage rate on a new purchase and the premium the North Shore market puts on move-in-ready homes, buying more space can cost far more than adding it to the home you already own and love. An addition also lets you keep your neighborhood, your school district and your yard, and a well-designed addition typically returns a strong share of its cost in added home value. We help you weigh the numbers honestly before you commit.

Can I live in my home during the addition?

In most cases yes, and we plan the project so you can. We isolate the work area with dust barriers, protect floors and stairs along the whole path, and phase the job so we do not open your existing home to the weather until the new structure is dried in. There are short stretches, when we tie the addition into your kitchen, break through an exterior wall or reroute utilities, that are noisier and more disruptive, and we schedule those tightly and tell you in advance. For a large second-story addition, some families choose to stay elsewhere for the framing phase, and we will give you a straight answer about what to expect for your specific project.

What is an ADU and are they allowed on the North Shore?

An ADU, or accessory dwelling unit, is a small independent living space with its own kitchen, bath and entrance, often used as an in-law suite, a space for an adult child, or a rental. Massachusetts has moved to make ADUs easier to build by right in single-family zones statewide, though each North Shore town still applies its own rules on size, parking and setbacks, and the details continue to evolve. An ADU can be attached as an addition, built above a garage, or created by converting existing space. We stay current on the local requirements and design the ADU to comply while fitting the character of your home and neighborhood.
