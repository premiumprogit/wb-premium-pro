Lead-Safe Painting in Older Revere Homes

A large share of Revere's homes were built before 1978, which means lead paint is a real possibility on the siding, trim and interior surfaces. This is not a reason to avoid painting, it is a reason to hire painters who prep the right way. Sanding or scraping old paint carelessly spreads lead dust through a home and a yard, and that is exactly the risk to families and tenants that we are careful to avoid.

We follow lead-safe work practices: containing dust, protecting the work area, and prepping older surfaces deliberately rather than blasting away at them. Beyond the safety, this careful prep is what makes the new paint last. Old, chalking, or loosely bonded paint is a bad foundation, and coating over it is why so many quick jobs peel within a year. Doing the prep correctly on an older home is slower up front and cheaper in the long run, because you are not repainting again next season.

Exterior Siding, Trim and Fascia on Revere Homes

A Revere exterior is not one surface, it is a stack of them, and each part fails differently in salt air. The siding takes the broad weather, the trim frames every opening and takes the water that runs off, and the fascia and soffit up under the roofline are where moisture collects and where peeling starts long before anyone on the ground notices.

Trim around windows and doors gets the extra pass it needs because it is the first thing a visitor sees and the first thing to show a sloppy edge. Power Washing and Surface Cleaning Before We Paint

Paint does not stick to salt, dirt, chalk or mildew, and near Revere Beach every exterior carries all four. That is why a real exterior job starts with washing, not with the first gallon of paint. Skipping it is the single most common reason a fresh coat lets go early on a coastal home.

We wash siding, trim and porch surfaces to strip off the salt film, the road grime and the mildew that builds up on shaded elevations, and we let the surface dry fully before any coating goes on. We match the pressure to the surface so older wood and softer trim are cleaned, not gouged. The result is a sound, clean surface the primer and paint can grab, which is the difference between a finish that holds through several coastal winters and one that starts flaking the first spring.

Wallpaper Removal and Wall Prep

Wallpaper is common in older Revere homes, and painting straight over it almost never works, the seams telegraph through, the paper bubbles, and the whole wall has to be redone within a year. Doing it right means taking the paper off and repairing the wall underneath before any paint touches it.

Only then does the new color go on. It is more work than painting over the paper, and it is the only way to get a wall that looks like plaster again instead of a patched-over shortcut.

Lead-Safe Practices on Pre-1978 Revere Properties

Because so much of Revere's housing predates 1978, lead-safe work is not a niche add-on here, it is the baseline for doing the job responsibly. The danger with old paint is almost never the paint sitting still on the wall, it is the dust created when someone preps it carelessly, and that dust is what settles into a home and yard and puts families and tenants at risk.

Our approach is to contain the work area, capture dust rather than spread it, and prep older siding, trim and interior surfaces deliberately instead of dry-sanding blind. That protects the people who live there and it protects the finish, because a controlled, sound surface holds paint far longer than one that was blasted clean. Do you do lead-safe painting in older Revere homes?

Many homes in Revere were built before 1978 and can contain lead paint, so surface prep has to be done carefully. We follow lead-safe work practices, contain dust, and prep older siding and trim the right way rather than sanding blind. This protects your family and any tenants and gives the new paint a sound surface to bond to, which is why the finish lasts on older homes instead of peeling in a season.
