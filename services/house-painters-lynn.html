Being house painters in Lynn, we run every job the way a general contractor runs any project, with one crew accountable for the whole outcome, the prep, the repairs, the paint and the cleanup, from the first estimate to the final walkthrough.

Interior Painting for Lynn Homes

Interior painting is the fastest way to make a Lynn home feel new, and it is where careful prep separates a professional job from a weekend one. We move and protect your furniture, mask floors, trim and fixtures, then fill nail-pops, sand rough spots, and spot-prime patches and stains so they do not flash through the finish coat. Only then do the walls, ceilings and trim get their coats, cut in by hand for sharp lines where the wall meets the ceiling and trim.

Older Lynn homes come with their own quirks, and there are a lot of them: plaster walls that need the right patching, ornate Victorian trim, crown and paneled doors that reward a steady hand, and layers of old paint that sometimes need proper sanding, priming or lead-safe handling to bond safely. As finish carpenters as well as painters, we handle those details in stride. We also color-match from any sample, chip or piece of your decor, and we are happy to advise on colors that photograph and age well in the light your rooms actually get. Tying wall, ceiling and trim colors into one plan is what makes a room read finished rather than just repainted. See our full interior painting service for more on how we work inside your home.

Exterior Painting for Lynn Victorians and Triple-Deckers

Exterior house painting in Lynn is really two jobs: the prep and the paint, and on an older, taller home the prep is the part that decides how long you go before you do it again. New England weather, plus Lynn's proximity to the coast, attacks the wood and the old finish, so we start by washing the surface, then scraping and sanding failed paint, treating and repairing bare or damaged wood, re-caulking joints, and spot-priming everything down to a sound, paintable base. On a triple-decker, that means proper staging and safe access to reach three stories of clapboard, porch trim and detail work the right way. Skipping any of that is exactly how a fresh coat ends up peeling within a season.

Only once the surface is right do we apply premium exterior paint rated for New England weather, in the number of coats the substrate needs for real coverage and durability. Because we are a licensed general contractor, the carpentry repairs that so many Lynn exterior jobs need, replacing a rotted board, rebuilding a soft porch column, fixing trim before it fails, are handled by the same crew, so there is no gap between the fix and the finish. We plan Lynn exterior work around the weather window, roughly late spring through early fall, so the paint cures the way it should. Our full exterior painting page covers the process in more detail.

Lead-Safe Painting on Older Lynn Homes

Because so much of Lynn's housing was built before 1978, lead paint is a real consideration on a lot of the homes we paint here, and it changes how prep is done. Old paint that is scraped, sanded or disturbed carelessly can spread lead dust through the home, so we follow lead-safe work practices: containing the work area, keeping dust down, collecting debris and cleaning up thoroughly. This protects your family, your neighbors in a shared building, and our crew, and it is the kind of thing a cut-rate painter often ignores. If your Lynn home predates modern paint standards, we talk you through what lead-safe prep means for the schedule and the scope before we start, so there are no surprises once the work is underway.

Why Prep Decides Whether a Lynn Paint Job Lasts

Almost every failed paint job, inside or out, traces back to prep, not paint. Indoors, patches that were not primed flash through the topcoat, and stains bleed back within weeks. Outdoors on an older Lynn home, paint applied over chalking, dirty or peeling surfaces simply will not bond, and moisture works its way behind it and lifts it off, faster on a tall triple-decker that catches wind and weather on every side. That is the number one reason a homeowner who tried a cheap crew or a DIY weekend ends up calling a general contractor to redo it properly.

Our prep is deliberately thorough, because thorough is what makes the finish last. Walls and trim are patched, sanded and spot-primed. Exteriors are washed, scraped, sanded, repaired, re-caulked and primed down to a sound base before a single finish coat goes on, and old paint is handled safely along the way. It is not glamorous work, and it is the part a homeowner never sees, which is exactly why it is the part that gets skipped by cut-rate crews and never skipped by us.

How Often Should You Repaint a House in Lynn?

Interior paint in a Lynn home generally lasts five to ten years before it looks tired, longer in low-traffic rooms and sooner in kitchens, bathrooms and the busy hallways and stairwells common in multi-family homes. Exteriors are a different story on an older, taller home near the coast. Weather and sun shorten the life of exterior paint here, so many Lynn homes need an exterior repaint every five to eight years, and tall triple-deckers with sun-facing exposures sometimes sooner. Good prep and premium coatings stretch that interval, which is one more reason the unglamorous prep work pays for itself over the life of the finish.
