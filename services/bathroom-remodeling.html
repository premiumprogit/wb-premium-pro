Bathroom waterproofing is the part that decides everything

If one thing separates a bathroom that lasts twenty years from one that fails in three, it is waterproofing, and it is invisible the day the job is finished. A shower rarely leaks where you can see it. Water finds the grout line, the corner where the wall meets the pan, or the spot where a valve passes through the tile, and then travels into the framing and stays there. By the time a stain shows on the ceiling below, the damage has been growing for months. We build showers on a waterproof membrane over a properly sloped base, seal every seam and penetration, use cement backer instead of drywall behind tile, and size ventilation to actually clear moisture. That is the part you never see and the part that decides whether the room lasts.

Walk-in showers and tub-to-shower conversions

The most requested bathroom project across the North Shore is converting a cramped tub-shower combo into a larger walk-in shower. We remove the old tub, reframe and waterproof the new base, run any plumbing changes and tile the walls and floor for a shower that is easier to use and easier to clean. If it is the only bathtub in the house, we talk through resale first, since many buyers still want at least one tub. For a full primary suite, we pair a curbless shower with a freestanding soaking tub, a double vanity and a heated tile floor.

Tile, vanities and the finishes that make the room

Good bathrooms are mostly good decisions made early. We help you choose tile, vanities, countertops, fixtures, lighting and paint that fit both the look you want and the way the room is used, then we source and install them. Large-format porcelain reads clean and cuts down grout lines, subway and herringbone patterns suit the older North Shore housing stock, and quartz or marble-look tops handle daily use. If you already have a designer or a Pinterest board, we build to it. If you do not, we guide the selections so they land on budget and on schedule.

How long a bathroom remodel takes

Most bathroom remodels take about two to four weeks on site. A straightforward tub-to-shower conversion or a refresh can finish in two weeks, while a full gut with plumbing relocation, custom tile and a primary suite layout runs three to four. Older North Shore homes sometimes add a few days once we open the walls and find dated plumbing or hidden water damage, and we flag that in the estimate before we start rather than surprising you mid-project.

Permits, inspections and Massachusetts code

Any bathroom remodel that touches plumbing, electrical or framing needs a permit, and plumbing and electrical work must be pulled by the licensed trades doing it. As a licensed Massachusetts general contractor, we handle the permits and coordinate the inspections so the job is done to code and documented for when you sell. Work done without permits can quietly become a problem the day you list the house.

What to avoid when remodeling a bathroom

Most bathroom remodels that go wrong go wrong in predictable ways, and almost all of them trace back to cutting a corner you cannot see. The biggest one is tile set over green board or plain drywall instead of a real waterproof system, which looks identical on day one and fails within a couple of years. Close behind it is a shower pan that was never sloped or sealed correctly, a fan that is either missing or vented into the attic, and plumbing or electrical work done without a permit by someone not licensed to do it. On the money side, the trap is a vague estimate with allowances so low that the real cost of tile and fixtures shows up as change orders later. We quote real numbers, put the scope in writing, do the waterproofing and rough-in by the book, and pull the permits, precisely so none of these become your problem after we leave. If a bid comes in dramatically lower than everyone else, ask what is being left out, because in a bathroom the cheap version is usually the expensive version on a delay.

One project lead is your single point of contact, so you are not chasing a plumber, a tiler and an electrician who never talk to each other. Small bathrooms, powder rooms and aging-in-place

Not every bathroom project is a full primary suite. A lot of North Shore homes have a tight hall bath or a half bath that just needs to work harder. In small bathrooms the layout decisions matter most: a wall-hung vanity or a pedestal sink buys back floor space, a curbless shower makes a cramped room feel bigger, and a pocket door frees up the swing of a hinged one. For homeowners planning to stay in the house, we build in aging-in-place features that do not read as clinical, such as blocking in the walls for future grab bars, a comfort-height toilet, a bench in the shower and a slip-resistant floor tile. Done up front, these cost very little and save a second remodel later.

Ventilation, lighting and the details that get skipped

The difference between a bathroom that feels finished and one that feels almost right is usually in the parts people forget to spec. Ventilation has to be sized to the room and vented outside, not into the attic, or you trade a fogged mirror today for mold tomorrow. Lighting works best in layers: a bright, color-accurate fixture at the mirror for grooming, recessed cans for the room, and a sealed, damp-rated light in the shower. We also plan outlets and a GFCI where you actually use them, run a dedicated circuit for heated floors if you want them, and set niche and bench heights around the people using the shower. None of it is glamorous, and all of it is why the room still works years later.

Bathroom remodeling FAQ

How much does a bathroom remodel cost on the North Shore?

Most bathroom remodels run $12,000 to $32,000. A cosmetic refresh lands around $12,000 to $18,000, a full gut with a walk-in shower, tile, vanity and fixtures runs $18,000 to $28,000, and a primary suite bathroom can reach $30,000 and up. Your written, itemized estimate is free.

How long does a bathroom remodel take?

About two to four weeks on site. A refresh or tub-to-shower conversion can finish in two weeks, while a full gut with plumbing relocation and custom tile runs three to four. Older homes occasionally add a few days once we open the walls.

Can you convert my tub into a walk-in shower?

Yes, tub-to-shower conversions are one of our most requested projects. We remove the old tub, reframe and waterproof the new base, adjust the plumbing and tile the walls and floor. If it is your only tub, we talk through resale first.

Will my bathroom leak or grow mold after the remodel?

Not when the waterproofing is done right. We build showers on a waterproof membrane and a properly sloped base, use cement backer behind tile, seal every seam and add correctly sized ventilation, so the bathroom stays dry behind the surface, not just on it.

Do I need a permit to remodel a bathroom in Massachusetts?

Yes, any remodel that touches plumbing, electrical or framing needs a permit, and those trades must be pulled by licensed professionals. As a licensed Massachusetts general contractor we handle the permits and inspections and build to code.
