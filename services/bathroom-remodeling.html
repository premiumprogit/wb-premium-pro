The Bathroom Remodel Process, Step by Step

A bathroom is a small room where the sequence matters enormously, because once the tile is set there is no going back to fix the plumbing or the waterproofing underneath it. This is the order we work in on a full bathroom remodel in Peabody, and it is exactly why one accountable crew running the whole job beats a chain of separate trades.

1. Demolition and protection. We protect your floors and the path through the house, then remove the old tile, fixtures, vanity and, on a full gut, the wall surfaces down to the studs and subfloor. Careful demo is where we first see the true condition of the plumbing, the framing and the subfloor in an older Peabody home.

2. Rough plumbing and electrical. With the walls open, our licensed plumber relocates or replaces supply and drain lines, and the licensed electrician runs the circuits, the fan and the lighting a modern bathroom needs. In older homes this is where undersized or corroded lines get replaced so you are not opening the wall again in five years.

3. Subfloor, framing and blocking. We repair or replace any soft or unlevel subfloor, add blocking for grab bars, a floating vanity or heavy fixtures, and make sure everything the tile and cabinetry sit on is solid and true. A bathroom built on a spongy floor telegraphs cracked grout within a season.

4. Waterproofing. Before any tile, we install a proven waterproofing system, typically Schluter Kerdi, on the shower walls, floor, curb or curbless pan and the wet areas. This is the single most important step in the whole project and the one that separates a bathroom that lasts from one that leaks.

5. Tile. Floor tile, shower surround, niche, accent bands and any feature wall are set, then grouted and sealed. Good tile is equal parts material and craft, and it is done by our crew rather than handed to an outside tiler on someone else's schedule.

6. Vanity, fixtures and finish. The vanity, countertop, sink, faucet, toilet, shower valve and trim, glass, mirror, lighting, exhaust fan and hardware all go in, then paint and final trim. We finish with a full walkthrough where anything that needs attention gets fixed before we call the job done.

Full Bath, Half Bath, Primary and Guest Bathrooms

Not every bathroom serves the same purpose, and the smartest remodel starts by matching the scope to how the room is actually used. A full bathroom, with a tub or shower, a sink and a toilet, is the workhorse of most Peabody homes and usually the one where a tub-shower combo makes the most sense for families and for resale. A half bath, or powder room, has only a sink and toilet, so a remodel there is more about finishes, a statement vanity, tile and lighting, than about plumbing and waterproofing, which keeps the cost down and the timeline short.

A primary or en-suite bathroom is where homeowners tend to invest the most, because it is the private, spa-like retreat off the main bedroom. Here we often build a large curbless or low-threshold walk-in shower, a double vanity, a soaking tub when the space allows, heated tile floors and premium finishes. A guest or hall bathroom sits in between: it needs to look great and function well for visitors and everyday use, but usually does not warrant the full luxury budget of a primary bath. We help you decide where each dollar earns its keep so the whole house feels considered, not just one room.

Tubs Versus Walk-In Showers and Aging-in-Place Options

One of the biggest decisions in any bathroom remodel is whether to keep a tub, install a walk-in shower, or combine the two. Keeping at least one tub in the home is a smart move for resale and for households with young children, so a clean tub-shower combo often earns its place in a main or guest bath. In a primary or en-suite bath, many Peabody homeowners are choosing to remove a rarely-used tub in favor of a large, luxurious walk-in shower with a bench, a niche and multiple heads, which reads as more modern and more usable day to day.

Walk-in showers are also the heart of aging-in-place remodeling. A curbless, zero-threshold shower with a linear drain, a built-in bench, grab bars set into properly blocked walls and a hand shower makes a bathroom safe and comfortable to use for decades, without looking clinical. We build these to look like a high-end spa rather than a hospital, and because we frame and block the walls during the rough stage, features like grab bars and future accessibility can be added cleanly rather than screwed into unsupported drywall later.

Waterproofing Systems and Why They Matter

Everything you see in a finished bathroom, the tile, the grout, the paint, is only as good as the waterproofing you cannot see behind it. This is the step that separates our bathrooms from a cheap remodel, and it is invisible the day the job is done. A shower that was tiled directly onto ordinary drywall, or over a failed pan, will wick water into the framing, the subfloor and the ceiling of the room below, and by the time you see a stain the damage is already expensive.

We use proven, manufacturer-backed waterproofing systems, most often Schluter Kerdi, applied to the shower walls, floor, curb or curbless pan and the surrounding wet areas before any tile is set. On floors we can add an uncoupling membrane that also protects against the small movements of an older Peabody subfloor, which is what prevents cracked tile and grout over time. Done right, waterproofing is the least glamorous and most important part of the whole project, and it is the reason our bathrooms are still dry and solid many years in.

Tile, Vanities, Countertops and Fixture Choices

The finish choices are where your bathroom becomes yours. On tile, porcelain and ceramic are the durable everyday workhorses, natural stone like marble adds a timeless, high-end look with a little more upkeep, and large-format tile means fewer grout lines and a cleaner, more modern feel. We help you match the floor, shower and any accent tile into one palette rather than a collection of separate decisions, and we handle the layout details, niches, benches, curbless transitions and trim, that make tile look custom instead of builder-grade.

Vanities range from a compact single sink in a guest bath to a full double vanity with drawers and a makeup station in a primary suite, in furniture-style, floating or built-in styles. Countertops in quartz give you a durable, low-maintenance surface, while natural stone offers character, and we pair them with the sink, faucet and hardware so everything agrees. On fixtures, the toilet, shower valve and trim, faucets, glass enclosure, mirror, lighting and hardware all get selected together so the finished room feels designed. Because our licensed plumber and electrician work on our schedule, these connections happen cleanly at the right stage.

Ventilation, Moisture Control and Small-Bathroom Solutions

A bathroom is the most humid room in the house, and moisture is the enemy of everything you just installed. A correctly sized, correctly vented exhaust fan that actually exhausts to the outside rather than into the attic is not optional, it is what keeps mold off your new paint and grout and protects the whole remodel over time. In older Peabody homes we frequently add or upgrade ventilation that was undersized or missing entirely, along with humidity-sensing controls so the fan does the work for you.

Small and awkward bathrooms are the norm in a lot of Peabody homes, and the right remodel is often less about square footage and more about smart layout. Wall-hung or floating vanities, a corner or curbless shower, a pocket door instead of a swinging one, recessed niches, a taller mirror and better lighting can make a tight bathroom feel dramatically larger. Where it makes sense, we can borrow a few inches from an adjacent closet or hall, or reconfigure the fixture layout so the room finally works, all planned before demo so there are no surprises.

Remodeling a Bathroom in an Older Peabody Home

Peabody has a lot of older housing stock, and that character is exactly what makes a bathroom remodel here more involved than in a newer subdivision. The most common things we find behind the walls of an older Peabody bathroom are galvanized or lead supply lines that are due for replacement, drain lines that were never sized for a modern fixture, undersized wiring with no dedicated circuit for the fan or lighting, plaster that crumbles the moment you open it, and a subfloor that has gone soft around an old toilet flange or tub.

A contractor who has not worked in these homes treats each of these as an emergency and a change order. We treat them as expected, budget realistically for them up front, and bring in the licensed plumber and electrician on our schedule so they are handled cleanly rather than in a panic mid-project. The result is a bathroom that looks brand new and, just as importantly, has modern plumbing, wiring, ventilation and a solid floor underneath the finishes, so you are not opening the walls again anytime soon. If your project grows beyond the bathroom, we can carry it into related work like drywall, insulation and plaster repair throughout the surrounding rooms.

That is not red tape to fear, it is the paper trail that protects you and your home's value at resale, and it is proof the hidden work behind the tile was done correctly. As your licensed general contractor we handle the permit applications, schedule the inspections and make sure every stage is signed off, so you are never chasing the city yourself.

Because we are a full general contractor, the licensed plumber and electrician work under our roof and on our schedule, not as separate contractors you have to hire, coordinate and chase. That is what lets the rough plumbing, the electrical and the inspections happen at exactly the right moment, before the walls close up, rather than becoming a delay that stalls the whole job. Knowing the local process is a quiet part of why our Peabody bathrooms run on schedule instead of getting hung up.
