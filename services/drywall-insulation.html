The Process

How Our Drywall Process Actually Works, Step by Step

Good drywall is a sequence, and skipping or rushing a step is what causes the cracked seams and visible patches you see on a bad job. Here is exactly how we finish a wall so it stays smooth for years.

01

Insulate and Hang

With the wall open we insulate to code, then hang the board with the right screw pattern and staggered seams so the wall is rigid and gaps are minimized before any mud.

02

Tape and First Coat

Seams and corners are taped and bedded in the first coat of joint compound. Tape is what holds the seam together, and skipping it is the number one cause of future cracks.

03

Three Coats of Mud

Two more coats of compound go on, each feathered wider than the last and each allowed to fully dry before the next. Three coats is what makes seams truly disappear.

04

Sand, Prime and Inspect

We sand to a flat, even surface, prime so the wall takes paint uniformly, and inspect under raking light to catch any ridge or low spot before we call it done.

Insulation Types and R-Values for a North Shore Home

The North Shore falls in climate zone 5, which means real winters and real heating bills, so the insulation choice is not just about filling a cavity. Different materials suit different parts of the house, and matching the right product and R-value to the right location is what separates a wall that holds heat from one that leaks it. Here is how we advise homeowners on the four types we install most, with typical uses and recommended R-values.

Insulation typeBest used forTypical R-value
Batt (fiberglass or mineral wool)Open walls, floors, sound control between roomsR-13 to R-21 in walls
Blown-in celluloseAttic floors, topping up existing attic insulationR-49 to R-60 in attics
Spray foam (closed cell)Rim joists, basement walls, tight air-sealingR-6 to R-7 per inch
Rigid boardContinuous exterior sheathing, basement walls, foundationR-5 to R-6 per inch

For most North Shore homes we recommend roughly R-49 to R-60 in the attic, about R-20 to R-21 in exterior walls where the framing allows it, and R-15 to R-19 on basement and crawlspace walls, in line with the Massachusetts energy code for our zone. Older homes are frequently far below those numbers, sometimes with no wall insulation at all, which is exactly why the upstairs is cold and the heating bill is high. When we open a wall for drywall work, topping up the insulation at the same time is the cheapest it will ever be, because you are not paying twice to open and close the same wall.

Plaster Repair and Skim Coating for Older North Shore Homes

A large share of the homes we work in, especially in Salem, Beverly and Marblehead, were built with plaster over wood or metal lath rather than drywall. Plaster is a beautiful, durable surface, harder and more sound-dampening than modern board, and it is worth preserving rather than tearing out on reflex. The problem is that after a century of settling, moisture and old repairs, plaster develops hairline cracks, sections pull away from the lath, and surfaces grow uneven. The good news is that most of that can be fixed without a full gut.

We re-secure loose plaster back to the lath with plaster washers or adhesive, fill and rebuild cracks so they do not simply reopen, and where a wall or ceiling is generally sound but tired we skim coat the entire surface to a smooth, flat, paint-ready finish. Skim coating is often the smartest money in an older home, because it gives you a fresh, modern surface while keeping the solid plaster and the character of the house. Where plaster is genuinely beyond saving, water-destroyed or crumbling over a large area, we cut out the failed section, insulate the cavity behind it if it is an exterior wall, install drywall to match the plaster thickness, and blend the transition so the finished wall reads as one continuous surface. Combined with our interior painting service, a skim-coated room can look completely renewed for a fraction of a full renovation.

Soundproofing Walls, Ceilings and Shared Spaces

Sound control is one of the most requested upgrades we handle during drywall work, and it is far easier to build in while a wall is open than to add later. Whether it is a home office where you need quiet for calls, a bedroom against a busy hallway, a media room, or a shared wall in a two-family North Shore home, the same principles apply. We add sound-dampening batt insulation in the cavity to absorb airborne noise, and for tougher situations we install resilient channel to decouple the drywall from the framing and a second layer of thicker board with a damping compound between the layers.

Because we handle the insulation and the drywall together, the soundproofing is done as one coordinated step rather than a bolt-on. We also seal the small gaps, around outlets, at the floor and ceiling, and where pipes or ducts pass through, that quietly leak most of the sound in a typical wall. The result is a noticeably quieter room without turning the whole house into a construction project.

Moisture and Mold Considerations in North Shore Basements

Basements are where a lot of North Shore homeowners want to add living space, and they are also where drywall and insulation go wrong most often when moisture is ignored. Our basements deal with humid coastal summers, spring groundwater and older foundations that were never sealed, and finishing over a damp foundation traps moisture where you cannot see it. That is how you get musty smells, ruined insulation and mold behind a brand new wall. So before we insulate or hang a single sheet of board in a basement, we deal with the water first.

That means checking for active leaks and grading or drainage issues, addressing the source rather than the symptom, and then building the wall assembly the right way for a below-grade space. We favor closed-cell spray foam or rigid board directly against the foundation, which resists moisture and creates an air and vapor barrier, over fiberglass batts that can absorb water and hold it against the wall. We keep wood framing off the concrete, and we use moisture-tolerant materials where they make sense. Doing it this way is the difference between a finished basement that stays healthy for decades and one that has to be torn out in a few years.

How Better Insulation Cuts Heating Bills Through a New England Winter

On the North Shore, heat is money, and an underinsulated home leaks it all winter. Most of that loss happens in two places: the attic, where heat rises and escapes straight through the ceiling, and the air leaks at the rim joist, the top plates, and around windows and outlets. You can run the furnace all day, but if the attic is at R-19 instead of R-49 and the rim joist is open to the cold, you are heating the outdoors. Bringing the attic up to modern R-values and air-sealing those hidden gaps keeps the heat you already paid for inside, which is why insulation is one of the highest-return upgrades on an older home.

Homeowners feel it in two ways. The bill drops, oil, gas or electric, because the system cycles less to hold the same temperature, and the house is simply more comfortable, with fewer cold rooms upstairs and fewer drafts. Because we do the insulation and the drywall together, we can air-seal properly before the wall is closed, which is the part a drywall-only crew or an insulation-only crew often misses. Do it once, do it right, and the winter after is noticeably warmer and cheaper.

Timelines: How Long a Drywall and Insulation Job Takes

Drywall has a natural pace because joint compound has to dry between coats, and that is a feature, not a delay, since rushing the mud is what causes problems later. A small patch or repair is usually one to two days. A single room, insulated, hung, taped, given three coats, sanded and primed, typically takes about four to seven working days. A plaster skim coat of a room runs a similar window depending on how many coats the surface needs. A whole-home drywall project as part of a renovation or addition runs several weeks and is scheduled around the other trades.

Insulation on its own is fast: an attic top-up or a blown-in job is often a day or less, and rim-joist spray foam is quick once the area is prepped. We give you a realistic schedule in your written proposal, sequence the insulation before the board so nothing has to be reopened, and hold to the timeline because one crew runs the whole job rather than waiting on a subcontractor who is busy elsewhere.

Permits and When They Apply

Not every drywall or insulation job needs a permit, and part of our job is telling you honestly when one applies so you are never caught out. A simple patch, a like-for-like drywall replacement, or a plaster skim coat generally does not require a permit. Larger work does: new walls, framing changes, drywall as part of a permitted remodel or addition, and insulation added under a state energy rebate or program all come with paperwork and, in some cases, an inspection. As your licensed general contractor we identify the requirement up front, pull the permit from your town, and coordinate any inspection so the finished work is to code and properly signed off. That paper trail protects you and your home's value at resale.

What R-value insulation does Massachusetts energy code require?

The North Shore sits in climate zone 5, and the Massachusetts energy code recommends roughly R-49 to R-60 in attics, about R-20 to R-21 in 2x6 exterior walls (or R-13 to R-15 plus a layer of continuous rigid board on 2x4 walls), and R-15 to R-19 for basement and crawlspace walls. Existing older North Shore homes are often far below these levels, which is exactly why heating bills run high through a New England winter. When we open a wall for drywall work we bring the insulation up to modern targets so you are not tearing the wall back apart later.

Do you repair old plaster walls or only install new drywall?

We do both. A huge share of North Shore homes in Salem, Beverly and Marblehead have original plaster and lath, and plaster does not have to be ripped out to be fixed. We re-secure loose plaster, fill and rebuild cracks, and skim coat entire walls and ceilings to a smooth, paint-ready surface that matches the rest of the room. Where plaster is too far gone we can replace a section with drywall and blend the two so the repair disappears. Preserving good plaster is usually cheaper and keeps the character of an older home.

Can better insulation really lower my heating bills?

Yes, and it is one of the highest-return upgrades on an older North Shore home. Most heat is lost through an underinsulated attic and air leaks at the rim joist, top plates and around windows. Air-sealing those gaps and bringing the attic up to R-49 or better keeps the heat you already paid for inside through the winter, which lowers your oil, gas or electric bill and makes the upstairs rooms far more comfortable. Because we handle both the insulation and the drywall, the air barrier is done right before the wall is closed.

How long does a drywall and insulation job take?

A single room, hung, taped, given three coats of mud, sanded and primed, typically takes about four to seven working days once demo and any insulation are done, because each coat of joint compound has to dry before the next. A patch or small repair is often one to two days. A whole-home drywall project runs several weeks. Insulation itself is fast: an attic top-up or blown-in job is usually a day or less. We give you a realistic schedule in your written proposal and hold to it because one crew runs the whole job.

Do I need a permit for drywall or insulation work?

A simple patch or a like-for-like replacement usually does not need a permit, but larger jobs that involve framing changes, new walls, or work tied into a bigger remodel typically require a building permit from your town, and insulation added under a rebate or energy program has its own paperwork. As your licensed general contractor we tell you up front when a permit applies, pull it, and coordinate any inspection so the work is done to code and signed off properly.
