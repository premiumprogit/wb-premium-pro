Exterior House Painting in Salem

Exterior painting is where Salem's age and its location on the water really test a contractor. Lead Paint and Older Homes: Why It Matters

If your Salem home was built before 1978, and most were, there is almost certainly lead-based paint somewhere under the current finish. That is not a reason to panic, but it is a reason to hire a contractor who takes it seriously. The danger with lead paint is not the intact surface, it is the dust created when old paint is scraped and sanded, which is exactly the prep a lasting exterior job requires. A crew that ignores it can spread lead dust through your yard and into your home.

As a licensed and insured contractor, we follow lead-safe work practices on pre-1978 homes: we contain the work area, use methods that limit dust, clean up thoroughly and dispose of debris properly. It is slower and more careful than a cut-rate crew's approach, and that is the point. You get the heavy prep that makes paint last, without the health risk that comes from doing that prep carelessly. We flag lead paint and any other surprises during the estimate, so it is priced into the plan from the start instead of becoming a mid-job change order.

Lead-Safe Painting for Pre-1978 Salem Homes

Because the vast majority of Salem's housing stock predates 1978, lead-based paint is the rule rather than the exception. The risk is not the intact painted surface, it is the fine dust released when old paint is scraped, sanded or power-sanded, which is exactly the prep a durable finish demands. A crew that rushes this can spread lead dust across your yard, your porch and into the rooms where your family lives.

We follow lead-safe work practices as a licensed and insured contractor: we contain the work zone with plastic and drop cloths, use methods that keep dust down, HEPA-vacuum and wet-wipe as we go, and bag and dispose of debris properly. We flag suspected lead during the estimate and price the safe handling into the plan from the start, so it never becomes a surprise change order once the ladders are up.

Power Washing and Surface Cleaning Before Paint

A lasting exterior finish starts with a clean, sound surface. Salem homes near the coast collect salt film, mildew, pollen and chalky oxidized paint that will keep a new coat from bonding if it is painted over. We wash siding, trim and porches with the right pressure and cleaners for the surface, knocking off dirt and mildew without driving water behind clapboards or gouging soft old wood. On homes with heavy lead paint we adjust the method so cleaning does not become an uncontrolled way to spread paint chips. Clean, dry, sound wood is what makes primer and finish coats grab and hold, and it is why we treat washing as real prep rather than a rushed rinse. Wallpaper Removal and Wall Preparation

Older Salem homes are full of layered wallpaper, sometimes several generations deep, over plaster that does not forgive shortcuts. Painting over old wallpaper almost always telegraphs seams and bubbles through the new color. We strip wallpaper properly, wash off the old adhesive, skim and sand the walls smooth, and prime before any finish coat, so the paint goes onto a clean, even surface. On horsehair plaster and older drywall we take the extra time to patch cracks and stabilize soft spots first, because the flattest, most durable paint job starts with the wall underneath it being right.

Trim, Door and Window Painting

On a period Salem home, the trim is where the craftsmanship shows, and where a careless job shows just as fast. Doors are painted for a smooth, even finish that opens and closes without sticking. This is finish carpentry-grade painting, not a quick pass with a wide brush.

Seasonal Timing: When to Paint in Salem

New England weather sets the calendar for exterior painting. In Salem, the reliable exterior season generally runs from late spring through fall, when temperatures and humidity let paint cure properly and coastal damp is easier to work around. We watch dew points and overnight temperatures, not just the daytime forecast, because paint applied on a surface that will sweat overnight does not bond well. Interior painting, by contrast, is a year-round job and winter is often the ideal time for it, since we can work comfortably indoors while the outdoor season is closed. Planning your project around the right window is part of getting a finish that lasts, and we help you schedule accordingly.

Coastal and Salt-Air Considerations

Salem sits right on the water, and that setting is hard on paint. Salt-laden air, driving nor'easters and constant moisture attack finishes faster than they would inland, especially on the ocean-facing sides of a home. We plan for it: thorough cleaning to remove salt film, spot-priming of all bare wood, careful caulking of joints where wind-driven rain gets in, and exterior-grade products chosen to flex and hold up in coastal conditions. On the most exposed elevations we may recommend a heavier prep or an extra coat, because the goal is a finish measured in years on the water, not one summer.

Our Prep Process, Step by Step

Prep is where a paint job is won or lost, and on an old Salem home it is most of the work. We start by protecting the site, covering floors, furniture, landscaping and hardscape. Then we clean the surfaces, scrape and sand failing paint with lead-safe containment on pre-1978 homes, and make carpentry repairs to rotted or split wood. We spot-prime bare and repaired areas, caulk gaps and joints, fill holes and cracks, and sand smooth so the finish has a sound surface to grip. Only then do the finish coats go on. It is slower than a wash-and-slap approach, and that is exactly why the result holds up.
