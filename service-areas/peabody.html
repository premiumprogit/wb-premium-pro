Working with Peabody's housing stock

Peabody is not one kind of house, and a good general contractor has to know the difference before the first demo day. West Peabody leans toward postwar ranches, split-levels and later colonials, many with finished basements, attached garages and additions layered on over the years. Closer to Peabody Square and South Peabody you find older two- and three-family homes, tighter lots and the kind of original framing and plaster that rewards patience and punishes a crew in a hurry. Along the Route 1 and Route 128 corridors there are updated colonials and larger homes where the goal is usually a higher-end kitchen, a primary-suite addition or a full exterior refresh. We adjust the plan to the house in front of us: how the framing was actually built, where the load paths run, how the older systems were pieced together, and what the resale expectations are for that street. That local read is exactly what a contractor driving in from another county does not have, and it is why our written scope tends to hold up once the walls are open.
