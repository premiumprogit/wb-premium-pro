Home additions in Marblehead

When a Marblehead family needs more room, an addition almost always beats moving, because there is nowhere else quite like this town to move to. But an addition on a coastal or historic home is the hardest thing a general contractor does here, and it is exactly where we focus. The goal is simple to say and hard to execute: the new space has to read as though it was always part of the house. That means matching the roof pitch and eave lines, carrying the siding and corner boards through, sizing new windows to the original proportions, and repeating the trim profiles instead of substituting whatever the lumberyard stocks. See our dedicated home additions in Marblehead page for types, permits and cost.

We handle the range Marblehead homeowners actually ask for: primary-suite additions over a garage or off the back, kitchen and family-room bump-outs that open a cramped older floor plan, mudrooms and entries that survive a Nor'easter, and full second-story additions on one-and-a-half-story capes and colonials. On lots near the water we detail the addition for salt air from the start, with flashing, drainage and materials chosen for exposure rather than for the cheapest bid. Because one contractor owns framing, systems and finishes against a single schedule, the addition ties into the existing house cleanly instead of announcing itself with a mismatched roofline. Explore home additions and framing for the structural side of the work.

Kitchen remodeling in Marblehead

Older Marblehead kitchens tend to be smaller and more compartmentalized than a new-build layout, so the win is usually in the plan, not just the finishes. We look at how the room actually connects to the rest of the house, whether a wall can come out, where the light comes from, and how to fit modern cabinets, counters and appliances into a footprint that was drawn a century ago. Then one crew handles cabinets, countertops, plumbing, electrical and finish carpentry on the same schedule, so the room lands finished rather than assembled. When a full rebuild is not the right call, cabinet painting and new counters can transform an older kitchen for a fraction of the cost.

Bathroom remodeling in Marblehead

Bathrooms punish shortcuts anywhere, and an older coastal home doubles the stakes. A remodel that looks fine on day one but was not waterproofed correctly turns into rot behind the tile, and in a Marblehead house that damage can reach original framing that is expensive to fix. We build baths the slow-is-fast way: sound substrate, proper waterproofing, a clean tile layout that respects the room's real dimensions, quality valves and fixtures, and ventilation that actually moves moisture out instead of trapping it in a tight older wall. One crew handles demo, plumbing, tile and finish so the schedule holds. See bathroom remodeling for a full scope.

Exterior painting, carpentry and salt-air protection

On the coast, the exterior is not cosmetic, it is protection. Marblehead homes take wind-driven rain, sun and salt that shorten the life of any coating that was rushed. We treat exterior painting as prep first: scraping, priming bare wood, repairing failing trim and flashing, then applying products chosen to hold up near the water. Our finish carpenters repair or replicate the trim, corner boards, doors and built-ins that give these homes their character, so a repaint does not quietly erase the detail. See exterior painting and carpentry & trim for what that includes.

Permits, historic review and Marblehead code

A lot of remodeling stress in Marblehead comes from the paperwork, not the building. As a licensed Massachusetts general contractor, we pull the right permits, build to current code, and coordinate inspections so nothing stalls halfway through. On additions and exterior changes we also account for the town's review process and, where it applies, the character of a historic district, so plans do not get bounced back weeks into the job. Work done without proper permits becomes a problem the day you sell, so we keep the record clean and let you think about the finished space instead of the file at Town Hall.

The best projects here start before the first swing of a hammer.
