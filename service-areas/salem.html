Working around Salem's older homes without the surprises

The difference between a smooth Salem remodel and a stressful one usually shows up in the first two weeks, when the walls come open and the house tells you what is really behind the plaster. Knob-and-tube wiring, undersized supply lines, out-of-plumb framing that has settled over a century, lead paint on original trim, and horsehair plaster that cracks the moment you touch it are all common in this city, and none of them belong in a homeowner's lap as a mid-project surprise. We plan for them. During the estimate we probe the areas we can reach, ask about the age of the systems, and build a realistic contingency into the written proposal so the number does not balloon the week demolition starts. When we do find something, you hear about it the same day, with options and a clear cost, not a vague change order at the end. That honesty up front is what keeps a Salem project on schedule and on budget, and it is the reason so many of our jobs come from a neighbor who watched the last one go right.

Older homes also reward matching over replacing. A colonial or Federal near Chestnut Street was built with proportions and trim profiles that a big-box replacement part will never match, so our finish carpenters mill and match rather than swap. Original hardwood floors that look tired are often better sanded and refinished than covered, and single-pane windows with good sashes can sometimes be restored instead of ripped out. We tell you when replacing is genuinely the right call and when it is just the easy one, because the goal is a home that keeps its character and its value, not a renovation that flattens what made you buy the house in the first place.
