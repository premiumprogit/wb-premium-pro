Remodeling Revere's triple-deckers and multi-families

A huge share of Revere's housing is multi-family: triple-deckers, two-families and converted homes where one owner lives in a unit and rents the rest, or where an extended family shares the building floor by floor. Remodeling those homes is a different discipline than working on a detached single-family in the suburbs. Shared walls carry sound and vibration, stacked plumbing stacks mean a bathroom on the second floor sits directly above the one on the first, and electrical service was often added to piecemeal over decades. When we take on a Revere multi-family, we plan for the whole building, not just the one room in front of us: we check how a new bathroom drain ties into the existing stack, whether the panel can carry a modern kitchen, and how to sequence the work so tenants or family in the other units are disrupted as little as possible. Because one accountable contractor owns the whole job, there is a single person tracking those cross-unit issues instead of a plumber, an electrician and a tile setter each assuming someone else handled it. That is how you avoid the classic Revere remodel that looks finished but leaves the neighbor below with a stain on their ceiling three weeks later.

Building for Revere's coastal climate the right way

Revere's location right on the Atlantic shapes every exterior and even some interior decisions. Salt in the air corrodes fasteners and hardware faster than inland, wind-driven rain finds any gap in flashing or siding, and the freeze-thaw swings of a New England winter work joints loose over time. On exterior painting, that means we do not skip the scrape, prime and caulk steps that make a coat actually bond, because a shortcut here fails in a single season near the water. On decks and railings, we use corrosion-resistant hardware and set posts properly so they do not heave. On additions and window work, we detail the flashing and air sealing so wind-driven moisture stays out of the wall. Even inside, humidity from coastal air makes bathroom ventilation and kitchen exhaust more important than people expect. These are not upsells, they are the difference between work that survives a Revere winter and work that starts failing the following spring. We build to that reality because we are going to be the ones you call if it does not hold, and referral-built means we would rather do it once, correctly.
